Roadside Vegetation Management

Importance of roadside vegetation management

Vegetation on a road corridor is a safety asset when it is controlled and a liability when it is not. Unmanaged growth hides signage and guideposts, closes down sight distance on curves and intersections, obscures wildlife and pedestrians, and stops drivers seeing the edge of the seal at night.

It also has consequences beyond visibility. Growth in table drains and culverts holds water on the pavement and shortens the life of the seal, standing dry fuel raises fire risk along the corridor, and roadsides are one of the main pathways weeds travel between properties. Managing it on a planned cycle — rather than reacting when complaints arrive — keeps drainage working, keeps fuel loads down and keeps crews out of live traffic doing hand work.

Do I need a forward-reaching reach mower?

A forward-reaching arm puts the head ahead of the tractor’s cab, directly in the operator’s natural line of sight, instead of behind the rear axle. On a busy road that is a real advantage: the operator can see the head, the obstacle and the traffic together, without working over their shoulder, and can react before the head reaches a guidepost, culvert headwall or tree.

It suits mixed work — trees, hedges, awkward corridors, frequent repositioning — and it is where the GreenTec Scorpion and Spider geometry earns its place.

What is the weight requirement for a 6.3 m reach arm?

A 6.3 m arm such as the Scorpion 630 is generally matched to tractors from about 5,000 kg. The number is a starting point, not the whole answer — what actually keeps the machine safe is the combination of tractor mass, wheel track width, tyre and ballast setup, and the linkage or subframe carrying the arm.

Reach works against you: the further the head goes out, the more the load tries to lift the opposite wheel, and that effect is worse on a crossfall, a soft shoulder or a batter. Counterweighting, wheel spacing and mounting position are all part of the specification, along with PTO speed for the pump-driven models. Send us the make, model, tyre setup and how you intend to work, and we will confirm whether the 6.3 m arm is the right one.

RX 133 slashing head vs FR 122 mulching head

The FR 122 is a 1.2 m flail head and the everyday tool for cyclic roadside work. It processes what it cuts into finer material and follows ground contour on its rear roller, which gives a tidy finish on grass, weeds and light thicket and keeps material on the verge rather than across the seal.

The RX 133 is a 1.3 m slashing head running a tri-blade rotor, built for heavy and woody vegetation, branches, stumps and stony ground where a flail mower takes a beating. It severs material rather than reprocessing it, so the finish is coarser. In rough conditions its maintenance is less than a standard flail mower. Most operations run the flail as the standard head and add the RX 133 for the heavy corridors.

Roadside tree maintenance

Tree work on a corridor is about maintaining a clearance envelope — over the traffic lanes, above signs and lighting, and back from the shoulder — on a cycle, before limbs become a hazard or an emergency job. The right head depends on branch diameter and the finish the road authority expects.

Rotary hedge cutters handle lighter branch material and shred it as they go. Cutterbars make a clean, controlled cut with limited projection, which matters in residential and high-traffic areas. QuadSaw LRS tree saws take the heavier limbs, and can be run from a tractor reach arm, a GreenTec Multi Carrier, a telehandler or an excavator. What decides the build is the reach and height you need, the branch size and the machine you already own.
